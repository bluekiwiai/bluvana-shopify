# Pose-Transfer Prompts — Pink Satin Reference Set

These prompts are designed for use with:
1. one of the six uploaded images as the **pose reference**, and
2. a separate **target image** whose model, outfit, scene, lighting, and visual identity should remain unchanged.

For every prompt below, the reference image should control **pose and body language only**.

---

## Pose 1 — Seated, One Hand on Thigh, One Hand Braced Beside the Body

Use the uploaded reference image **only as a guide for the seated pose, torso angle, leg placement, hand placement, head direction, and framing**.

Preserve everything else from the target image: keep the target model's identity, face, hairstyle, hair color, skin tone, body proportions, outfit, accessories, footwear, environment, furniture, background, lighting, lens perspective, and overall photographic style unchanged.

Reposition the target model into a pose closely matching the reference:

- Seat the model upright on the available chair, bench, bed, stool, or other existing support in the target scene.
- Keep the torso tall but relaxed, with the upper body angled slightly rather than perfectly square to the camera.
- Let the shoulders sit naturally and slightly asymmetrically.
- Position the hips securely on the seat with a subtle lean toward one side.
- Extend both thighs diagonally away from the torso rather than straight toward the camera.
- Keep the knees separated by a natural, moderate amount.
- Place one hand flat or softly relaxed on the top/outer area of the near thigh.
- Spread the fingers naturally along the thigh without gripping.
- Place the opposite hand beside the hip on the seat, armrest, or adjacent support surface.
- Keep that supporting wrist neutral and the elbow softly bent.
- Turn the head slightly to one side.
- Keep the chin neutral to subtly raised.
- Direct the eyes off-camera in the same direction as the head turn.
- Maintain a composed, relaxed fashion-editorial expression.
- Preserve a graceful seated silhouette with an elongated torso and relaxed shoulders.

Match the reference's **upright seated posture, diagonal thigh placement, one hand resting on the thigh, opposite hand braced beside the hip, sideward head turn, and calm body language** as closely as possible.

**POSE TRANSFER ONLY.** Do not copy the reference person's face, hairstyle, hair color, skin tone, lingerie, jewelry, chair, wall, lighting, room, colors, or photographic treatment.

If the target scene has no chair or seated surface, use an existing suitable support rather than importing the reference chair.

Avoid floating hands, bent or collapsed wrists, duplicated fingers, legs intersecting unnaturally, excessive back arching, or changing the target outfit to imitate the reference.

## Pose 2 — Three-Quarter Back View with Hand Near the Shoulder

Keep the target model's identity, facial features, hair, skin tone, body proportions, clothing, accessories, background, lighting, and camera style exactly as they appear in the target image.

Reposition the target model into this pose:

- Rotate the body away from the camera into a clear three-quarter back view.
- Keep the back and one shoulder prominently visible.
- Let the hips remain slightly angled rather than square to the camera.
- Shift the body weight primarily onto one leg.
- Allow the opposite leg to extend slightly outward or behind to create a natural asymmetrical stance.
- Bend one arm upward.
- Bring that hand gently toward the opposite shoulder, collarbone, or upper shoulder area.
- Keep the fingertips light and relaxed rather than pressing or gripping.
- Let the raised elbow point outward and slightly forward, creating a graceful curved line.
- Allow the other arm to remain relaxed along the far side of the body.
- Turn the head back toward the camera side, showing the face in side profile or soft three-quarter profile.
- Keep the chin neutral to slightly lowered.
- Direct the gaze downward or softly off-camera.
- Keep the shoulders relaxed and the spine natural.
- Preserve an elegant over-the-shoulder fashion pose rather than an exaggerated twist.

Match the reference's **three-quarter back orientation, hand-near-shoulder gesture, outward elbow, subtle hip shift, and side-profile head turn** as closely as possible.

**Do not transfer any appearance or styling from the reference.** Do not copy the reference model's face, hair, pink satin garment, straps, lace, jewelry, wall, furniture, room, or lighting.

Avoid over-rotating the neck, dislocating the shoulder, twisting the spine unnaturally, fusing fingers into the shoulder, or changing the target garment's back design.

## Pose 3 — Three-Quarter Front Pose, One Hand on Surface, One Hand Near Shoulder

Preserve the target model's identity, facial structure, hairstyle, body proportions, outfit, accessories, environment, lighting, background, and visual style unchanged.

Reposition the target model as follows:

- Angle the torso approximately 20–35 degrees away from directly front-facing.
- Shift the hips subtly to one side.
- Let one leg carry slightly more weight than the other.
- Extend one arm downward and outward toward an existing nearby surface such as a table, counter, dresser, chair back, or wall edge.
- Rest that hand lightly on the surface for balance.
- Keep the supporting arm nearly straight but not locked.
- Bend the opposite elbow and raise that hand toward the shoulder or upper collarbone area.
- Keep the raised hand hovering lightly near the shoulder, with softly curved fingers.
- Do not grip clothing or hair unless that is already natural in the target image.
- Tilt the head slightly downward and toward the raised hand.
- Direct the eyes downward or toward the hand rather than into the camera.
- Keep the expression composed, soft, and editorial.
- Allow the shoulders and hips to remain slightly uneven in a natural way.

Match the reference's **three-quarter body angle, one hand resting on a nearby surface, opposite hand lifted near the shoulder, gentle downward head tilt, and soft off-camera gaze**.

**POSE ONLY.** Do not copy the reference model's identity, hairstyle, body appearance, pink satin lingerie, lace, jewelry, wall color, furniture, lighting, or photographic styling.

If no surface exists in the target image, let the lower arm hang naturally rather than inventing furniture.

Avoid floating hands, fingers clipping through surfaces, stiff elbows, overly dramatic hip shifts, or changing the target garment to accommodate the pose.

## Pose 6 — Full-Body Hand-on-Hair Pose with Opposite Arm Down

Use the uploaded reference image **only as a guide for the full-body stance, raised-arm geometry, hand placement, leg positioning, head tilt, gaze, and overall body language**.

Keep all target-image appearance and styling unchanged: identity, facial features, hairstyle, hair color, skin tone, body proportions, outfit, accessories, footwear, environment, furniture, background, lighting, and camera treatment.

Reposition the target model into the following pose:

- Stand in a relaxed full-body fashion stance.
- Keep the torso mostly facing the camera with a small natural angle.
- Shift most of the weight onto one leg.
- Let the opposite leg extend slightly outward with the knee softly bent.
- Keep the feet naturally separated and stable.
- Raise one arm overhead.
- Bend the raised elbow clearly outward to create a triangular or curved silhouette.
- Place the raised hand gently on the top or side of the head/hair.
- Keep the fingers relaxed and naturally integrated with the hair without merging or disappearing.
- Let the opposite arm hang naturally beside the body.
- Keep the lower hand relaxed near the outer thigh or side of the garment.
- Do not grip or lift the garment unless the target clothing naturally allows a very light touch.
- Tilt the head slightly toward the raised arm.
- Lower the gaze toward the floor or off-camera.
- Use a soft smile or pleasant, relaxed expression.
- Keep the shoulders low and avoid shrugging the raised side.
- Preserve a long, natural line through the torso.

Match the reference's **one arm raised to the hair, outward elbow, opposite arm lowered, asymmetrical leg stance, slight knee bend, downward gaze, and soft smile** as closely as possible.

**POSE TRANSFER ONLY.** Do not copy the reference person's face, hair, pink satin outfit, lace, earrings, chair, room, wall, flooring, lighting, or color palette.

If the target hairstyle does not support a hand-through-hair gesture, rest the hand lightly on the top or side of the head without changing the hairstyle.

Avoid malformed raised arms, fingers fused into the hair, overly bent wrists, extreme hip shifts, warped knees or feet, or changes to the target outfit.

## Universal Pose-Lock Reinforcement

Append this paragraph to any prompt if the generator starts copying the reference model, clothing, or scene:

> **STRICT POSE TRANSFER ONLY. Treat the reference image as a skeletal/body-language guide, not as an appearance, wardrobe, or environment reference. Transfer only joint positions, limb angles, weight distribution, torso orientation, hand placement, head angle, gaze direction, and stance. The TARGET IMAGE is the sole source of identity, face, facial structure, hair, skin tone, body appearance, clothing, accessories, footwear, setting, furniture, background, lighting, camera perspective, texture, and color. Do not import, recreate, blend, or imitate any garment, lace, hairstyle, jewelry, furniture, scenery, lighting, or color palette from the pose reference.**

## Optional Anatomy Reinforcement

Append this if the image model struggles with hands, arms, or legs:

> Keep human anatomy natural and physically plausible: exactly two arms, two hands, five fingers per hand, two legs, and two feet. Preserve believable shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, and ankle articulation. No duplicated limbs, merged fingers, floating hands, twisted joints, warped feet, impossible crossings, or asymmetrical limb lengths.
