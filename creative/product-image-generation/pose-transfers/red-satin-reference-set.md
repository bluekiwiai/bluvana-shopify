# Pose-Transfer Prompts — Red Satin Reference Set

These prompts are designed for use with:
1. one of the six uploaded images as the **pose reference**, and
2. a separate **target image** whose model, outfit, scene, and visual identity must be preserved.

For every prompt, the reference controls **pose and body language only**.

---

## Pose 1 — Elegant Cross-Step, Head Turned to the Side

Use the uploaded reference image **only as a guide for the model's pose, posture, weight distribution, arm placement, head direction, gaze, and full-body composition**.

Preserve the target image exactly in all other respects: keep the target model's identity, facial features, hairstyle, hair color, skin tone, body proportions, clothing, accessories, footwear, environment, background, lighting, camera angle, lens characteristics, and visual style.

Reposition the target model into a pose closely matching the reference:

- Stand in a graceful full-body fashion pose with the torso mostly facing the camera, rotated only slightly into a three-quarter angle.
- Shift the weight primarily onto the rear/supporting leg.
- Bring the opposite leg forward and slightly across the body's centerline, creating an elegant cross-step.
- Keep the forward knee relaxed rather than locked.
- Let the forward foot land lightly and naturally, with the toes oriented mostly forward.
- Keep the hips subtly offset rather than perfectly square.
- Extend one arm outward to the side at approximately waist-to-lower-chest height.
- Bend that elbow softly so the forearm reaches outward with a relaxed, slightly downward wrist.
- Keep the hand loose and elegant, with naturally curved fingers rather than a rigid pointing gesture.
- Let the opposite arm hang naturally alongside the body, with a slight bend at the elbow.
- Turn the head clearly to one side, approximately 35–50 degrees away from the camera.
- Direct the eyes off-camera in the same direction as the head turn.
- Keep the chin neutral to slightly raised.
- Maintain a composed, confident, fashion-editorial expression.
- Keep the shoulders low and relaxed and the torso elongated.

Match the reference's **crossed walking stance, asymmetric arm placement, soft extended hand, sideward head turn, off-camera gaze, and poised upright posture** as closely as possible.

**POSE TRANSFER ONLY.** Do not copy the reference person's face, hair, makeup, body appearance, skin tone, red satin clothing, robe, jewelry, room, sofa, floor, lighting, color palette, or photographic treatment.

If the target outfit does not contain a loose outer layer, **do not add one**. Reproduce the skeletal pose and body language without inventing garments or fabric movement.

Avoid stiff wrists, claw-like fingers, exaggerated hip thrusting, impossible leg crossing, distorted feet, extra fingers, changed clothing, or a dramatic runway stride.

## Pose 2 — Dynamic Three-Quarter Back Turn with Arms Extended

Use the uploaded reference image **only for pose, body orientation, arm geometry, head turn, leg placement, weight distribution, and composition**.

Keep the target model's identity, face, hair, body proportions, skin tone, outfit, accessories, background, lighting, camera perspective, and overall aesthetic unchanged.

Reposition the target model into a dynamic pose closely matching the reference:

- Rotate the body away from the camera into a strong three-quarter back orientation.
- Keep the hips turned mostly away while allowing the upper torso to twist slightly so the shoulders are not completely square to the back.
- Place most of the weight on one leg.
- Extend the opposite leg diagonally outward/backward, with the foot lightly contacting the floor and the heel either lifted or barely weighted.
- Create a sense of turning or mid-motion without making the pose look unstable.
- Extend one arm outward and slightly forward from the body.
- Bend that elbow so the forearm travels across the side/front space at around chest height.
- Keep that hand relaxed with the palm angled naturally and fingers softly separated.
- Extend the opposite arm outward behind or to the side at a slightly lower height.
- Keep the second elbow gently bent rather than locked.
- Turn the head back over the near shoulder so the face is visible in three-quarter profile.
- Direct the eyes toward the camera or just past it.
- Keep the chin level and the neck long.
- Use a composed, subtly confident expression.
- Preserve a graceful twist through the torso without excessive spinal rotation.

Match the reference's **three-quarter back orientation, asymmetric outstretched arms, diagonal trailing leg, over-the-shoulder head turn, and sense of fluid mid-turn movement**.

**Do not transfer any appearance or styling from the reference.** Do not copy the reference model's face, hair, skin tone, red satin outfit, robe, jewelry, room, couch, architectural elements, lighting, or colors.

If the target outfit has no flowing layer, do not create one. The motion should come from the **body pose itself**, not invented fabric.

Avoid impossible shoulder rotation, dislocated-looking arms, twisted wrists, floating hands, fused fingers, excessive back arching, or warped legs and feet.

## Pose 3 — Full Back View with Over-the-Shoulder Look and Crossed Ankles

Use the uploaded reference image **only as a guide for the full-body pose, back-facing orientation, arm placement, head turn, ankle crossing, and posture**.

Preserve the target model's identity, facial characteristics, hair, body proportions, clothing, accessories, environment, lighting, camera style, and all other target-image details.

Reposition the target model as follows:

- Stand with the back almost fully facing the camera.
- Keep the torso upright and elongated.
- Let the shoulders remain level and relaxed.
- Allow both arms to hang slightly away from the body rather than pressed tightly against the sides.
- Keep both elbows very softly bent.
- Position the hands near the outer thighs/hips with relaxed wrists and naturally separated fingers.
- Place one leg as the primary supporting leg.
- Cross the opposite leg behind or slightly across the supporting leg at the ankles.
- Let the crossed foot rest lightly on the ball of the foot or toes, with the heel visibly raised.
- Keep the ankle crossing elegant and anatomically clear.
- Turn the head back over one shoulder so part of the face is visible.
- Keep the gaze directed back toward the camera or slightly past it.
- Maintain a neutral-to-softly-confident expression.
- Keep the spine natural; do not exaggerate the lower-back curve.

Match the reference's **full-back composition, softly separated arms, crossed ankles, lifted heel, and over-the-shoulder glance** as closely as possible.

**Do not copy the reference outfit, robe shape, red fabric, hairstyle, face, body appearance, jewelry, sofa, wall, floor, lighting, or color treatment.**

If the target has no robe or long outer garment, preserve the pose without adding one.

Avoid tangled feet, merged ankles, incorrect heel orientation, over-rotated neck, asymmetrical limb lengths, extra fingers, or exaggerated body reshaping.

## Pose 4 — Front-Facing Forward Step with Both Arms Open

Use the uploaded reference image **only for pose, stance, arm placement, forward-step geometry, head position, gaze, and framing**.

Preserve everything from the target image except the pose: the target model's identity, face, hair, body proportions, skin tone, outfit, accessories, footwear, location, background, lighting, lens perspective, and photographic style must remain unchanged.

Reposition the target model into this pose:

- Face the camera almost directly, with only a minimal natural torso angle.
- Keep the chest open and shoulders relaxed.
- Place one leg slightly forward toward the camera, as though taking a slow fashion step.
- Bring the forward leg close to the centerline so the stance feels narrow and elegant rather than wide.
- Keep the rear leg supporting most of the weight.
- Let both arms extend slightly outward from the sides of the body.
- Keep both arms angled downward rather than horizontal.
- Bend the elbows minimally so the arms look relaxed and fluid.
- Allow the hands to remain open with softly curved fingers.
- Keep the wrists neutral and natural.
- Hold the head upright and face forward.
- Direct the eyes toward the camera.
- Keep the chin neutral.
- Use a calm, confident, polished expression.
- Create the impression of gentle forward movement while keeping the pose controlled and balanced.

Match the reference's **front-facing composition, narrow forward step, subtly opened arms, relaxed hands, direct gaze, and poised forward-moving body language**.

**Do not transfer the reference model's appearance, red satin garments, robe, hair, makeup, jewelry, furniture, room, lighting, or colors.**

If the target outfit does not have a flowing outer layer, do not invent one or flare the clothing artificially. Preserve the original outfit's normal structure and drape.

Avoid overly wide arms, rigid elbows, symmetrical mannequin stiffness, exaggerated walking motion, warped legs, changed garment shape, or direct copying of the reference styling.

## Pose 5 — Three-Quarter Power Pose, Hand on Hip, Opposite Knee Bent

Use the uploaded reference image **only as a pose reference for body angle, hand placement, hip position, leg geometry, head direction, and overall full-body stance**.

Keep the target model's identity, face, hair, body proportions, skin tone, clothing, accessories, background, lighting, and visual style exactly as they are in the target image.

Reposition the target model into the following pose:

- Turn the torso slightly into a three-quarter angle while keeping the face oriented toward the camera.
- Shift most of the body weight onto one straight supporting leg.
- Move the opposite leg outward and forward.
- Bend that knee clearly, creating an open triangular shape between the legs.
- Extend the bent leg's foot diagonally outward, with the toes contacting the floor lightly.
- Keep the supporting foot planted naturally.
- Place one hand firmly but elegantly on the waist/hip.
- Angle that elbow outward to create a confident fashion silhouette.
- Let the opposite arm travel downward toward the front/side of the body.
- Place the lower hand near the upper thigh or near the garment's lower edge.
- If anatomically and stylistically appropriate for the target outfit, the lower hand may lightly pinch a tiny amount of existing fabric; otherwise simply rest it near the thigh without altering the clothing.
- Keep the fingers long, relaxed, and anatomically correct.
- Keep the shoulders relaxed.
- Hold the head upright with a slight natural tilt.
- Look directly toward the camera.
- Use a poised, confident expression with a subtle smile or neutral editorial look.

Match the reference's **three-quarter body angle, hand-on-hip silhouette, outward elbow, bent side leg, diagonal toe placement, and direct-camera confidence**.

**Do not copy the reference model's face, hair, red slip dress, robe, jewelry, body appearance, sofa, walls, floor, lighting, or color palette.**

Do not pull down, open, shorten, or otherwise redesign the target outfit to imitate the reference. If there is no robe, do not add one.

Avoid extreme knee angles, floating feet, distorted toes, clawed fingers, an overly arched back, excessive hip reshaping, or garment deformation.

## Pose 6 — Relaxed Front Stance with One Hand at the Upper Torso

Use the uploaded reference image **only as a guide for pose, stance width, hand placement, head position, gaze, and body language**.

Preserve the target model's identity, facial features, hairstyle, body proportions, skin tone, outfit, accessories, footwear, setting, lighting, camera perspective, and overall visual style.

Reposition the target model into a relaxed, elegant full-body stance:

- Face the camera almost directly.
- Keep the feet separated by a comfortable, moderate distance.
- Shift the weight slightly more onto one leg to avoid a perfectly symmetrical stance.
- Let the opposite leg relax outward slightly.
- Keep both knees soft rather than locked.
- Allow one arm to hang naturally down alongside the body.
- Keep that lower hand relaxed near the outer thigh, with naturally separated fingers.
- Bend the opposite elbow and bring that hand gently toward the upper torso.
- Place the raised hand near the collarbone, upper chest, neckline, lapel, or upper edge of the target garment—whichever is naturally available in the target outfit.
- The hand should lightly touch or hover near the existing clothing; it should not pull the garment open or change its design.
- Keep the raised wrist neutral and the fingers softly curved.
- Hold the head upright with a very subtle tilt.
- Direct the eyes toward the camera.
- Use a calm, approachable, softly confident expression.
- Keep the shoulders down and the torso long and relaxed.

Match the reference's **front-facing stance, moderate foot separation, slight weight shift, one arm relaxed down, opposite hand raised to the upper torso, and direct pleasant gaze**.

**Do not transfer the reference model's face, hair, body appearance, red robe, slip dress, jewelry, room, sofa, flooring, lighting, or colors.**

If the target outfit does not have a lapel or robe edge, place the raised hand naturally near the collarbone or upper chest **without inventing new clothing**.

Avoid pulling the neckline, exposing additional skin, stiff fingers, crooked wrists, perfectly symmetrical legs, distorted feet, or altering the target outfit.

## Optional Anatomy Reinforcement

If the generator struggles with hands or legs, append:

> Keep human anatomy natural and physically plausible: exactly two arms, two hands, five fingers per hand, two legs, and two feet. Preserve believable shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, and ankle articulation. No duplicated limbs, merged fingers, floating hands, twisted joints, warped feet, or impossible crossings.
