# The Shower Filter — image and video shot list

Every slot on the new PDP, in order, with generation prompts. Use the proven recipe: 2 or 3 real Pinterest reference photos as image anchors, one hard motivated light source, no hands or faces where avoidable, and the standard negative block (no CGI surfaces, no HDR, no invented text, no watermark). The gallery is a swipeable sales presentation, so each slide has one job.

## Product gallery (replaces the Temu images, in this order)

1. **Hero render — "this is premium."** Polished chrome inline shower filter standing upright on a warm cream plaster sweep, photographed straight on with an 80mm lens look, one large soft key from the left, a true contact shadow, one crisp specular line down the chrome. Nothing else in frame. This is the slide that kills the plumbing-aisle objection.

2. **In context — "it belongs in a nice bathroom."** The filter mounted between a shower arm and a chrome showerhead in a sage vertical-tile shower, faint steam, hard window light raking the tile. Filter fills a third of the frame, sharp; room falls soft.

3. **The water itself — "the outcome."** Water sheeting off a forearm and shoulder in a shaft of hard light, droplets crisp, steam behind, deep shadow around. No face. This is the sensory promise: softer-feeling water.

4. **Cartridge cutaway — "the mechanism."** Macro of the white cartridge cut in half lengthwise on slate, its layered media visible as distinct strata, cool window light, shallow depth. IMPORTANT: order a sample first and match the real layer structure; do not invent media the cartridge does not contain.

5. **What hard water leaves behind — "the culprit."** An old chrome showerhead crusted with white limescale beside a new clean one, side by side on dark slate, one hard raking beam. The honest before-and-after we can actually show.

6. **The three styles — "choose your setup."** Inline filter, multi spray showerhead and luxe flow showerhead lined on one cream sweep with even spacing, one directional shadow each. This slide does the work of a comparison chart.

7. **Install — "no plumber."** Tight crop of the filter threading onto a shower arm, one hand entering frame from below barely visible at the edge, or hands cropped out entirely with the filter half-threaded. Steam-free, bright, practical.

8. **Cartridge trio — "the rhythm."** Three white replacement cartridges stacked in a loose column on the cream sweep, top one tilted, soft shadow.

9. **Scale and fit — "it will fit."** The filter beside a standard shower arm with a yellow fabric measuring tape loosely coiled nearby on plaster, hard afternoon light, honest shadows. Echoes the homepage finder flat-lay language.

10. **Finish macro — "quality up close."** Extreme close-up of the polished chrome shoulder with three real water beads, each with a window catchlight, background falling to black.

## Section media inside the page

- **Culprit band video slot** (16:9, 8 to 10 second muted loop): steam drifting through a shaft of light in a dark shower, water hitting tile in slow rhythm, one slow push in. Moody, underexposed edges. No people.
- **Install strip images** (three, 4:3): step one, a showerhead being unscrewed (hand cropped at wrist or absent, head mid-turn); step two, the filter half-threaded on the bare arm; step three, the finished stack with water running. Same bathroom, same light, so they read as one sequence.
- **Cartridge band image** (4:3): a used grey-tinged cartridge beside a new white one on slate, hard side light. The honest "this is why you replace it" frame.

## Notes

- Chrome is the hero finish; keep every scene's metal consistent.
- No text, badges, or graphics inside any image. All words live in the page, where we control them.
- The used-versus-new cartridge and limescale shots are our proof workhorses; they show the mechanism without making a single medical claim.
